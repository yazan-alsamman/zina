# Art Direction Decision Matrix

**Status:** Phase 2 self-assessment of the selected direction, *The Testing Room*.

Scored 1–10 against the fifteen criteria in the brief. Every score is explained, and the honest
scores are the low ones — a matrix where everything scores 9 is marketing, not evaluation.

**Overall: 8.3 / 10.** Two criteria score 6, and both are known, named risks rather than surprises.

## 1. Luxury perception — 8

Luxury here comes from restraint, space, typography and photography rather than from ornament: a
warm ink ground, a matched bi-scriptual serif, 96–128px section rhythm, full-bleed imagery, and a
near-total absence of interface.

**Not 10**, deliberately. The direction refuses the loudest luxury signals — gold, gloss, ornament,
cinematic 3D, dramatic reveals. A visitor primed by luxury-fashion websites may read it as
*serious* before reading it as *expensive*. That is the intended trade: this is a publication, not a
maison, and being mistaken for the latter would undercut the positioning.

**Not lower**, because every conventional premium cue that survives scrutiny — space, type,
photography, discipline — is present and heavily invested in.

## 2. Beauty relevance — 8

The palette is drawn from pigment and skin rather than from metal or from the category's default
pink. The warm ground was chosen partly so that medium-deep warm skin tones render truthfully, which
matters on a site whose stated expertise is shade accuracy. Photography is macro, tactile, texture-led.

**Not 10** because the direction deliberately avoids the immediate visual signals of beauty —
softness, pink, gloss, glamour. Someone scanning a list of beauty sites might not identify it as one
in the first second. The compensation is that it will not be *confused* with any of them either.

## 3. Personal-brand relevance — 9

The design is built around one person's practice: her method, her observations, her limits, her
photography, first-person copy. The homepage headline is her claim, not her name — which is a
stronger personal-brand statement than a large signature would be.

**Not 10** because the person is currently mock. Every bio, portrait and statistic is a placeholder,
and the direction's intimacy depends on photography that does not yet exist (R-04).

## 4. Editorial quality — 9

Fixed section order so two reviews can be read side by side; a real measure; four hierarchy levels
separated by role; index and plate language borrowed from print; hairlines instead of cards;
alternating reading and scanning density. It behaves like a publication rather than a page.

**Not 10** until it exists in the real typefaces. Editorial quality is finally a typographic
judgement, and the type is specified but unset.

## 5. Trust — 10

The highest score, and the criterion the direction was built around.

Disclosure above the hero with prominence scaling to commercial entanglement. Limitations at
identical weight to strengths. Testing conditions published in a printed-table treatment.
Observations timestamped and bound to numbered plates. Dated update logs, never hidden. Method
stages showing which were *not* applied. `doesNotProve` rendered at the same weight as purpose. No
score, no stars, no chart, no laboratory iconography.

The design's argument and the content's argument are the same argument. That is rare, and it is why
this is the one 10.

## 6. Arabic quality — 8

Arabic is structurally co-equal, not accommodated: a matched bi-scriptual display and body family;
size and leading factors as system tokens; tracking forbidden as a token rather than as a note; a
designed answer to the metadata problem; the margin index mirroring; bidi isolation specified; the
header sized to Arabic width; an Arabic screen-reader pass in the verification plan.

**Not 9 or 10 because none of it is proven.** No Arabic type has been set — Zarid is commercially
licensed and nothing is installed. The specification is sound and the visual proof is outstanding
(Q-1). Criterion 10 of the bilingual test — *"the Arabic page should not look like a translation"* —
can only be judged by a native reader looking at real type.

This is the score most likely to move, in either direction.

## 7. English quality — 9

A high-contrast wedge serif at generous scale, a proper measure, tracked small caps for metadata, a
mono record tier. English is the script the direction was drafted in and it fits comfortably.

**Not 10** for the same reason as §4: unset type.

## 8. Review-page quality — 10

Designed first, as instructed, and it is the strongest artefact of the phase. Seventeen sections
each with weight, width, type, spacing, surface, motion, mobile and Arabic behaviour specified. The
margin index, the plates, the conditions well, the three voices and the verdict band all have a
native home. Eleven template states enumerated from real fixture data. A defined page rhythm.

It reads as an editorial investigation rather than a product card, which was the brief's own test.

## 9. Homepage quality — 7

**The lowest score, and the honest one.**

The homepage is good: an asymmetric opening, proof on the second screen, the Method as a typographic
index, one CTA. But it is the surface where this direction is *least* naturally strong. The evidence
language — the thing that makes the review page distinctive — is rationed to a single block here,
because more would turn a reading surface into a dashboard. What remains is a well-executed dark
editorial homepage, which is a category with a lot of good work in it.

It also carries the most risk: it depends almost entirely on one portrait that does not exist, and
it is the page most likely to be judged in five seconds by someone deciding whether the project was
worth its budget.

**Mitigation:** proof on screen two is a genuine structural differentiator versus the usual
three-screens-about-me. If the homepage disappoints in Phase 3, the fix is more evidence, not more
decoration.

## 10. Professional / brand appeal — 9

The evidence layer *is* the pitch. A brand marketer sees published conditions, dated corrections and
a paid review that still says the twelve-hour claim did not hold — and understands both what they
would be buying and on what terms. The `/work/` cross-link to the independent review of the same
brand is the decision moment identified in Journey B.

**Not 10** because `/work/` inherits the reading register rather than getting a bespoke treatment,
and because empty results states — likely to be common — are specified but not yet designed in
detail.

## 11. Accessibility — 9

24 contrast pairs measured, not assumed; a real failure found and fixed. Every text pair clears AA,
nine clear AAA. The three-voice system survives greyscale and forced-colors because it uses
typeface, rule style and indentation as well as colour. Focus is designed and instant. No weight
below 400. Reduced motion loses nothing. The densest content on the site is fully static. Ten
art-direction decisions were taken *for* accessibility and are listed so they are not later
"improved".

**Not 10** because nothing is tested — no screen reader, no keyboard walkthrough, no forced-colors
pass, because no code exists. And there is no light theme, which some readers genuinely need (Q-4).

## 12. Performance — 9

Cheap by construction rather than by compromise. No 3D, no video, no glass, no parallax, no
animation library, no icon library, no carousels, no charts — roughly 200KB–5MB avoided. Depth comes
from hairlines and space, which cost nothing. The evidence layer, the most distinctive part of the
design, costs zero JavaScript. The review page has the second-lowest JS budget on the site.

**Not 10** because fonts are the one genuinely expensive decision: four families across two scripts,
requiring disciplined subsetting and locale splitting to stay inside 180KB per locale. And the
homepage mono question is unresolved.

## 13. Scalability — 9

The system is five signature devices, two densities, four hierarchy levels and two accents. It
extends to 500 reviews without new components, because a review page is the same shape every time —
which is the point of a fixed section order. Empty and partial states are designed rather than
discovered. Semantic tokens mean a light theme or a palette change is a value swap.

**Not 10** because at ~200 reviews the design needs things it does not yet have: on-site search,
category landing treatments, and an index dense enough to browse rather than scroll.

## 14. Distinctiveness — 9

Very little in the category has a margin index, numbered evidence plates, a published conditions
table, a three-voice type system, or a dark warm-ink ground. Roughly three in four beauty sites use
a pale foundation, so the ground alone separates it. The archival-annotation device — contact sheet
and condition report rather than dashboard — is, as far as the Phase 0 research found, unused in
this category.

**Not 10** because dark editorial minimalism is itself a recognisable mode, and the *homepage* in
particular will look familiar to anyone who follows design. The distinctiveness lives in the review
page, which is the right place for it but not the first place a casual visitor looks.

## 15. Timelessness — 8

Deliberately built to age: no glassmorphism, no neon, no gradient meshes, no 3D, no parallax, no
rounded-card convention, no animation library. The reference points — printed indexes, plate
captions, contact sheets — are decades old and stable. Type, space and photography age better than
effects.

**Not 10** because dark-mode-as-brand is itself somewhat of-the-moment, and the mono-as-data device,
while used here for a genuine reason, is currently fashionable and may read as dated in five years.

**Mitigation:** both are token-level. Semantic naming means the ground can become light and the
record tier can change family without touching structure. The *architecture* of the direction — the
three voices, the index, the plates, the fixed section order — is what will still be right in 2035.

## Summary

| Band | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **10** | Trust, Review-page quality |
| **9** | Personal-brand, Editorial, English, Professional appeal, Accessibility, Performance, Scalability, Distinctiveness |
| **8** | Luxury perception, Beauty relevance, Arabic quality, Timelessness |
| **7** | Homepage quality |

**Where it is strongest:** exactly where the brief said it must be — the review page, and trust.

**Where it is weakest:** the homepage, and the unproven Arabic. Both are named risks with owners.
The Arabic score is the one that can still move materially, and it moves as soon as the type is
licensed and set.
